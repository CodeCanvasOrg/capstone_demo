Securing a Scalable Platform

HumbleBeeAI delivered the multi-tenant architecture for LumioHub AI, an enterprise workplace platform designed for organizations with complex sites and access needs. [PDF p. 1] Read the case study and LumioHub AI project page.

Executive Summary

LumioHub AI addresses the architectural demands of a workplace platform serving enterprise clients: separate customer data, enforce permissions across complex organizational structures, and support AI inference that can scale independently. HumbleBeeAI reports delivering a resilient multi-tenant platform using schema-per-tenant isolation, hybrid role- and attribute-based permissions, and a dedicated AI recognition service. [PDF p. 1] A separate LumioHub AI project page lists its status as “prod ready” and provides a demo URL; demo availability has not been confirmed in the reviewed record. [PDF p. 1]

Context and Challenge

The platform was intended for enterprise clients with branches, departments and different levels of oversight. The central challenge was restricting a branch manager to relevant branch data while allowing an owner broader organizational access, without weakening tenant separation. The case study describes the need to design the distributed architecture from scratch and says a monolithic approach would not meet the stated security and scalability requirements. [PDF p. 2]

The available material does not document a specific pre-project operational workflow or quantify its costs. LumioHub’s product description separately frames workplace operations as a domain where organizations may rely on manual attendance, access cards, spreadsheets and ad-hoc supervision; those are product-context statements, not measured findings about this enterprise client. [PDF p. 2]

Solution and Capabilities

Tenant isolation and access control

The delivered design uses a PostgreSQL schema per tenant and a centralized middleware layer for hybrid RBAC and ABAC. Roles establish broad permissions; branch identifiers further limit access. Login tokens carry role and permitted branch IDs, and the middleware restricts list queries to those branches. These controls address the requirement to separate tenant data and apply permissions consistently across the platform. [PDF p. 3]

Services for workplace and AI functions

The service-oriented design separates a Node.js API, a Python face-recognition microservice and a React interface, with REST for synchronous requests and Redis for asynchronous messaging. The case study identifies independent scaling of the resource-intensive recognition component as a purpose of this decomposition. The product page describes workplace attendance, access visibility, presence analytics and reporting as LumioHub AI capabilities; it does not establish that every listed capability was delivered as part of this architecture engagement. [PDF p. 3] [PDF p. 1]

Results and Business Impact

The case study reports that the architecture delivered data isolation and scalable operation, and that branch-based permissions prevented cross-branch access. These are qualitative source-reported outcomes, not independently verified test results. No throughput, capacity, latency, security benchmark, audit report, scale-test result or quantified operational impact is provided; test conditions and comparison basis are therefore unavailable. [PDF p. 3]

Conclusion

This engagement established the reported architecture for LumioHub AI’s multi-tenant workplace platform, combining tenant-level data separation, role- and branch-aware permissions, and a separately scalable recognition service. The evidence supports those design and delivery claims, but not a quantified security, performance or business-impact result. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 3]
