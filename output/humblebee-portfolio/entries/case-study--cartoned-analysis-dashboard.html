How Johnson & Johnson Turned Endless Data Work into Instant Strategy?

A custom Cartonet Analysis Dashboard automated data processing and comparative product analysis for Johnson & Johnson’s marketing team, aiming to reduce time spent preparing and interpreting data. [PDF p. 1] View the original case study.

Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson’s marketing team needed to make sense of complex product-performance data, including analysis involving diagnostic catheters. Manual cleaning and fragmented analysis tools consumed analyst time and delayed access to findings. HumbleBeeAI describes building the Cartonet Analysis Dashboard to automate processing, statistical analysis and reporting. The case study reports an 80% reduction in manual effort; its PDF text has been reviewed, while owner approval remains pending. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 2] Original case study.

Context and Challenge

Previously, analysts spent hours preparing large datasets, cleaning and calculating results before they could use them. Data scattered across multiple tools made it difficult to build a unified view of product or physician-group performance. The case study says this fragmentation and slow identification of findings could delay campaign or strategy decisions. It does not provide the specific tools, data volumes, baseline hours, or detailed measurement conditions. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 2]

Solution and Capabilities

Automated analysis

The dashboard ingested raw datasets and applied statistical models automatically, producing ready-to-use insights intended to show which products or physician groups performed better. This replaces some manual data preparation and calculation with automated analysis. [PDF p. 2]

Interactive comparisons and reporting

Interactive visualizations, including box plots and summary tables, let marketers filter by product or physician, compare performance, and explore patterns. The case study also describes centralizing datasets for side-by-side comparisons, reducing the need to switch between platforms. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 3]
