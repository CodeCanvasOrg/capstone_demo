Investigating the Effect of Traffic Sampling on Machine Learning-Based Network Intrusion Detection Approaches

Investigating the Effect of Traffic Sampling on Machine Learning-Based Network Intrusion Detection Approaches
A published study examines how packet sampling affects flow-based machine-learning intrusion detection and its ability to identify malicious network traffic. [PDF p. 1]

Executive Summary

This research addresses network practitioners and researchers assessing machine-learning network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) when commodity flow-export devices sample packets rather than process every packet. Jumabek Alikhanov, Rhongho Jang, Mohammed Abuhamad, David Mohaisen, Daehun Nyang and Youngtae Noh published the paper online on 23 December 2021; it appeared in IEEE Access volume 10 (2022), DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2021.3137318. It is a research publication, not evidence of a HumbleBeeAI client delivery or product; no such relationship is established in the supplied sources. Context and Challenge

Flow-based NIDS models commonly rely on flow features generated from packet traffic. On commodity switches and routers, processing and exporting flow information can be constrained by traffic volume, memory and CPU demand, making packet sampling a practical compromise. But sampling may omit packets or entire short malicious flows, so systems evaluated on complete traffic may not behave the same way on sampled records. The study asks how sampling choices affect detection rate and false alarm rate. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 1]

Technical Approach

The authors propose a flow-level evaluation procedure intended to remain reliable when sampling or flow-export configuration changes the number of available records. They sample packet-capture traces from the CIC-IDS-2018 dataset, derive sampled flow records, and evaluate classifiers against flow-level ground truth. The comparison covers four samplers—Simple Random Sampling, Fast Filtered Sampling, SketchFlow and Sketch-Guided Sampling—and three classifiers: decision tree, random forest and a convolutional neural network. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 17]

Implementation

The work implements an experimental evaluation, not a production NIDS or customer integration. Its procedure compares sampled-flow model results while using flow-level evaluation to address differences in exported-record counts. The paper reports that source code for its sampling and machine-learning experiments was released; the provided evidence does not establish deployment, operational support or integration with a HumbleBeeAI product. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 1]

Evaluation

The evaluation tests sampling rates 1/10, 1/100 and 1/1000 across the four samplers and three classifiers. Reported metrics are detection rate and false alarm rate; figures express these as percentages and use a majority aggregation metric for flow-level detection. The study reports that sampled traffic can lose 20%, 50% and 80% of flow visibility at rates 1/10, 1/100 and 1/1000, respectively; loss of visibility means the NIDS cannot analyze the affected flow. These measurements are specific to the study, not universal network estimates. The supplied evidence does not give complete test conditions such as trace split details, repetitions, confidence intervals or hardware, so measurement reproducibility and generalization cannot be assessed here. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 17]

Results and Business Impact

Technically, the authors report that short malicious flows can go unnoticed even at sampling rates 1/10 and 1/100. Across the evaluated settings, SketchFlow generally performed better than the nonlinear samplers, and random forest paired with SketchFlow produced the reported favorable combination of higher detection and lower false alarm rates relative to other tested sampler-classifier combinations. The paper notes an exception: Sketch-Guided Sampling had a marked performance drop when the rate changed from 1/100 to 1/1000. No business outcomes, customer results or quantified operational savings are reported. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 20]

Limitations and Next Steps

The findings are bounded by the studied dataset, samplers, classifiers and three sampling rates; they should not be generalized to all networks. The paper also discusses constrained flow-cache conditions, but the supplied material does not provide a basis for translating its findings into production performance guarantees. No HumbleBeeAI-specific implementation, roadmap or next steps are documented. Further test conditions and independent validation are not provided in the reviewed evidence; no additional work is claimed here. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 2]

Conclusion

The paper offers an evidence-based comparison of packet-sampling choices for machine-learning flow-based intrusion detection. Its central practical caution is that sampling can hide malicious traffic before a classifier sees it, while the reported SketchFlow and random-forest combination is a promising result within the evaluated settings—not a demonstrated product or client outcome.
