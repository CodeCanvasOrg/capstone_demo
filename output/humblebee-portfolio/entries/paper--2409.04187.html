LITE: A Paradigm Shift in Multi-Object Tracking with Efficient ReID Feature Integration

LITE integrates appearance-feature extraction into multi-object tracking to reduce the overhead of separate ReID models.

Executive Summary

LITE is a research paradigm for tracking systems that need to maintain object identities across video frames, including surveillance, transport and sports-analysis settings. The paper asks whether ReID features can be integrated into detection and tracking without separate-model overhead. This entry concerns research, not a HumbleBeeAI product or client deployment: no product relationship is established in the supplied evidence. The paper, “LITE: A Paradigm Shift in Multi-Object Tracking with Efficient ReID Feature Integration,” is by Jumabek Alikhanov, Dilshod Obidov and Hakil Kim; it is an arXiv preprint v2 dated 1 October 2024, with publication approval pending. HUMBLEBEE R&D support is explicitly acknowledged. [PDF p. 1] Read the publication or PDF.

Context and Challenge

ReID-based trackers commonly use separate networks for object detection and appearance features, adding inference and preprocessing/post-processing costs. LITE addresses this added computation while retaining appearance cues for identity association. The source also notes that tracker speed varies with detector model, image resolution, detection threshold and scene density, so speed comparisons depend on configuration. [PDF p. 3]

Technical Approach

Rather than running an external ReID network, LITE extracts appearance features from detector feature maps within the tracking pipeline. The described YOLOv8m-based flow uses detected bounding boxes to rescale and crop activations, then reduces their spatial size into ReID features. LITE:DeepSORT replaces DeepSORT’s external ReID module; the paper also applies the paradigm to DeepOC-SORT and BoTSORT. [PDF p. 8] [PDF p. 7]

Implementation

For LITE:DeepSORT, the authors adapted the StrongSORT/DeepSORT implementation and replaced the external ReID module with LITE. They also adapted tracker repositories to follow their proposed holistic, real-time evaluation framework. The paper describes the implementation and benchmark experiments; production deployment or a client implementation is not established. The paper says code will be available post-publication at the project repository; current code availability is not verified here. [PDF p. 7] [PDF p. 1]

Evaluation

The reported benchmark result for LITE:DeepSORT on MOT17 is HOTA 43.03% at 28.3 FPS. The paper reports approximately twice DeepSORT’s speed on MOT17 and four times its speed on MOT20, with similar accuracy. These are research benchmark measurements, not service-level guarantees. Evaluation covers MOT17 and MOT20, with broader experiments also named for PersonPath22, KITTI and VIRAT-S. Detector, framework and configuration affect results; the supplied evidence does not specify a complete hardware setup, input-resolution workload or timing protocol for these headline numbers, so direct reproduction and comparison are limited. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 3]

Results and Business Impact

Technically, the reported results indicate that integrated appearance features can reduce tracking-time overhead while retaining similar benchmark accuracy. The paper does not report measured business outcomes, deployment savings or customer impact. Its findings should therefore be read as research evidence, not as results from a delivered HumbleBeeAI product. [PDF p. 1]

Limitations and Next Steps

Performance depends on detector choice and evaluation configuration, and the supplied record does not provide complete conditions for reproducing the headline speed figures. Owner approval and HumbleBeeAI portfolio review are pending. The paper identifies future work: apply LITE to other ReID-based trackers, investigate when ReID components help across scenarios, and evaluate LITE trackers on edge devices; these are research directions, not confirmed company plans. [PDF p. 3]

Conclusion

LITE presents a benchmarked approach to ReID-enabled multi-object tracking that reuses detector features instead of relying on a separate appearance model. Its reported speed and HOTA results support further evaluation, but do not establish production readiness or business impact. [PDF p. 1]
