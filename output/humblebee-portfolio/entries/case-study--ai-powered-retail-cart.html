How Retail AI solutions provider boosted accuracy by 20% with R&D

HumbleBeeAI’s R&D improved cart and item detection for a European retail technology provider, with reported mAP@50–95 increasing from approximately 55% to approximately 75%. [PDF p. 1]

Executive Summary

This project addressed detection challenges for an unnamed European company that builds intelligent checkout and loss-prevention systems. Its existing model struggled to identify shopping carts and a broad “item” category covering varied purchasable goods. HumbleBeeAI reports that focused computer vision R&D raised detection performance from approximately 55% to approximately 75% mAP@50–95. This is a review draft: the case-study PDF has been reviewed, while owner approval and portfolio feedback remain pending. Read the original case study. [PDF p. 1]

Context and Challenge

The client’s previous detector was reported at approximately 55% accuracy. Detection was difficult because “items” spanned products with many shapes, sizes, materials and colors, and performance varied across retail environments and lighting. The source also describes prior accuracy assessments as fluctuating with test-data composition, making reliable comparison harder. These constraints mattered for checkout monitoring, where missed cart or item detections could leave transactions untracked. The client serves varied retail settings, from local stores to large supermarket chains. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 2]

Solution and Capabilities

Detection focused on difficult retail items

HumbleBeeAI custom-trained a single-stage detection network with emphasis on the complex item class, aiming to learn visual features across varied retail scenarios. The work was specialized R&D for the client’s custom computer vision system. [PDF p. 2]

More diverse data and iterative refinement

The training dataset was expanded and diversified using real-world retail footage. Augmentation included scaling, random cropping, color and brightness variation, and geometric transformations. The team also analyzed error distributions and confidence variance across object categories to refine data sampling and model architectures. [PDF p. 2]

Confidence interval-based evaluation

A confidence interval-based evaluation methodology was introduced to make performance reporting more stable under dataset noise and domain variability. The case-study page describes real-time performance on edge devices, but supplies no measured FPS or hardware details; this entry therefore does not treat real-time performance as an independently quantified result. Results and Business Impact

The reported detection metric, mAP@50–95, improved from approximately 55% to approximately 75% for the existing and improved cart/item detector—about 20 percentage points, not a 20% relative increase. The source characterizes this as validated improvement and reports recognition across thousands of item types, but the test-set size, split, hardware, test conditions and confidence interval values are not provided in the reviewed record. The measurement is a model-performance result; no quantified business impact is supplied. Claims of reduced shrinkage, more efficient checkout monitoring or improved loss prevention are not accompanied by measured outcomes in the provided evidence. [PDF p. 2]
