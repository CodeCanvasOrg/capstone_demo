# Bojhona: AI-Powered Customs Intelligence: HS Code Definitions Bojhona brings HS-code definitions and historical customs product descriptions into one AI-assisted classification experience.  [PDF source: b94f3b26-9ba0-4e5e-bf4f-787e7d5aeaa2]

## Executive Summary

Bojhona is an in-development customs intelligence project for connecting the formal structure of Harmonized System (HS) codes with historical product descriptions from customs declarations. Its intended users include customs officers, managers, importers and exporters. The project page identifies its status as “in dev” and scope as “Mid”; no demo URL is provided. [View the original project page](https://humblebee.ai/projects/bojhona). [PDF source: 03b2b5f5-689d-402c-b8e4-e5004cf97b18] [PDF source: 72c6bda1-055c-4787-b912-5b390a549d4d]

The source describes the aim as helping users find classification context in one place rather than navigating legal definitions and practical trade data separately. Although the project write-up reports operational benefits, independent evaluation details and test conditions have not been provided.

## User Need

Classification requires applying legal HS-code definitions to the varied descriptions used for actual goods. The project account says customs officers searched large numbers of records manually, while duplicates and inconsistent descriptions complicated results; theoretical code definitions and practical customs-case data were also siloed. Importers and exporters were described as relying on consultants and brokers to interpret codes. [PDF source: 3bfd32fd-cb58-41c9-a1a7-1803a6ec4d1b] These are needs and challenges as reported in the project source, not independently measured findings.

## Product Vision

The intended direction is a shared classification experience where users can consult both the HS legal framework and examples drawn from historical product descriptions. The source describes integrating Phase A (official and structural HS-code definitions) with Phase B (historical product descriptions from customs declarations). No additional roadmap or future commitments are provided. [PDF source: afa03214-65cc-4fde-ad82-634e5000d2bc] [PDF source: 72c6bda1-055c-4787-b912-5b390a549d4d]

## Capabilities and Experience

The project write-up describes a data pipeline that cleans, normalizes and deduplicates customs product descriptions, then processes records in batches for semantic retrieval. It reports use of OpenAI text-embedding-3-small, Pinecone, MongoDB, FastAPI and LangChain with GPT models. The described chatbot returns HS-code suggestions with supporting context from definitions and product examples; the system is also described as comparing results and surfacing the top 10 relevant HS codes. The current website status remains “in dev,” so the write-up does not establish that all described functionality is generally available. [PDF source: 386cb6b6-585f-4eca-a97b-86df776585c8] [PDF source: 03b2b5f5-689d-402c-b8e4-e5004cf97b18]

The source describes the underlying dataset as “65.5+1.8 million” product descriptions on one page and “65.5M+” on another. It reports search and classification time reduced “from minutes to seconds,” but supplies no test conditions, baseline details or measurement method. These are project-reported technical and operational claims, not independently validated business-impact results. ## Differentiators

The supported distinction is the combination of structured HS definitions with historical product descriptions in a single classification workflow. The source also describes citation-backed answers and side-by-side comparison of candidate codes. It provides no comparative evidence against other products, so no superiority claim is made.
