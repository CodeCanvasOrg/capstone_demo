# How Johnson & Johnson reduced patient data analytics time by 80%?

Patient Report Analyzer helped Johnson & Johnson address inconsistent Excel-based patient data workflows with adaptive parsing, data cleaning and batch processing. [PDF source: 78bb7334-7237-4866-8a7c-2168cf401e13]

## Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson analytics teams faced delays turning patient datasets into usable analysis because Excel files varied in structure and required manual cleanup. HumbleBeeAI’s Patient Report Analyzer was presented as a way to automate parsing and cleaning and support analysis of varied datasets. The case study reports time and effort improvements, but provides no baseline hours, evaluation sample or test environment; its PDF text has been reviewed, while owner approval remains pending. ## Context and Challenge

The prior workflow relied on analysts to interpret files whose columns, sheet layouts and formats could differ. Parsing became a manual, file-by-file task; cleaning and reshaping consumed time and introduced opportunities for error. Large and complex spreadsheets also slowed processing, leaving analytics teams and healthcare users waiting for results rather than working directly with insights. The case study identifies these issues as the challenge addressed, but does not quantify the affected team size or provide baseline processing times. [PDF source: 40297078-7351-40d8-95a2-8719d559e0c7]

## Solution and Capabilities

### Adaptive parsing and cleaning

The Patient Report Analyzer was described as adapting to renamed columns and restructured sheets, then cleaning inconsistencies automatically. Its purpose was to reduce dependence on manual preparation across differently structured patient datasets. ### Batch processing and analysis outputs

Batch processing and caching were used to support runs on large files. The case study also describes charts, tables and downloadable Excel summaries, intended to make processed data easier to review and use. These are capabilities described in the case study; no separate production-status or deployment details are provided. [PDF source: 8f21d28b-6ad8-44cb-a8d8-b3d6339e1266]

## Results and Business Impact

The case study reports an 80% reduction in preprocessing/manual work compared with the earlier manual Excel workflow. It separately reports a 50% processing improvement, phrased in different places as processing time halved and as processing becoming 50% faster. These are source-reported claims, not independently validated results. The comparison denominator, baseline times, evaluation sample and test environment are not provided, so the two processing formulations should not be treated as a fully specified benchmark. The source describes handling gigabyte-scale files and reports a data-size figure of 1 GB+. This is a stated data-handling scale, not a timed benchmark or evidence of broader business impact. The case study attributes more analyst time for insight work and improved confidence to the change, but supplies no separate measured patient-care or business outcome.
