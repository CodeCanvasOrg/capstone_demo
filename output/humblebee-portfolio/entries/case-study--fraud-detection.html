A Global Beverage Leader Prevents Retail Fraud with AI

HumbleBeeAI integrated AI fraud detection into a retail distribution platform. [PDF p. 1]

Executive Summary

This project addressed the risk of field agents submitting false or reused compliance evidence in retail audits. HumbleBeeAI integrated fraud detection into an unnamed retail technology provider’s cloud distribution platform, which serves more than 600 customers across industries including FMCG and beverage. The case-study page headline refers to a global beverage leader, but its body describes the platform provider; the client attribution therefore remains unconfirmed. This entry is based on the source-reviewed case study, with owner approval pending: A Global Beverage Leader Prevents Retail Fraud with AI [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 2]

Context and Challenge

Field teams manually audited product displays across thousands of retail locations. The source describes reports containing images of device screens, miniature reference displays and closed coolers, making audit evidence unreliable. Manual verification at this scale was labor-intensive and costly; inconsistent field data also constrained merchandising and retail-execution decisions. The affected platform users included distributors and manufacturers relying on field agents to verify in-store execution. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 2]

Solution and Capabilities

Integrated fraud checks

HumbleBeeAI delivered an AI fraud-detection system integrated with retail field-report and planogram workflows. Its described checks target three forms of suspect evidence: a classification model detects images captured from a phone or tablet screen; a separate model identifies closed cooler doors; and an OCR-based algorithm examines product-label text to identify miniature or printed reference images. Together, these checks support validation of submitted visual evidence within the distribution platform. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 3] [PDF p. 3] [PDF p. 3]

Results and Business Impact

The case study reports fraudulent-submission detection accuracy of over 90% within the first week and an improvement in audit reliability of more than 90%. These are source-reported figures, not independently validated results. The source does not provide the test sample, fraud prevalence, error breakdown, reliability definition or denominator, nor a clear comparison basis for the reliability improvement. Business outcomes such as reduced costs or improved merchandising were not quantified in the reviewed material. [PDF p. 4]

Conclusion

The delivered integration applies computer vision and OCR to flag several documented types of invalid retail evidence within field-reporting workflows.
