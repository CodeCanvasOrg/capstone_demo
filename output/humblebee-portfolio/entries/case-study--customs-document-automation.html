Automating Customs Documentation with Local AI and Computer Vision

Executive Summary

HumbleBeeAI developed a locally hosted document-processing MVP for an unnamed logistics and customs brokerage firm. The project addresses manual transcription of invoices, packing lists and identity documents into customs declaration templates, while keeping sensitive data within the client’s infrastructure. The source describes a functional MVP and a separate roadmap toward fuller automation; this entry is a review draft, with owner approval pending. View the original case study [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 2]

Context and Challenge

Staff previously transcribed shipment details from physical or scanned documents into declaration templates. The work required focused attention and was described as slow and difficult to scale as document volumes grew. Invoice and packing-list layouts also vary by supplier and country, making basic OCR insufficient for consistently identifying meaningful fields. [PDF p. 2]

The documents can contain sensitive personal information, including PIN codes and identity numbers. The client required processing on internal infrastructure rather than sending this information to cloud AI services. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 3]

Solution and Capabilities

Local processing for sensitive records

OCR engines and LLM services run on the client’s local infrastructure. The source says document data stays within the organization’s perimeter. Lightweight models were selected to run on on-premise hardware without cloud compute or expensive GPU infrastructure. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 3]

Document understanding and editable outputs

Computer vision analyzes uploaded images and PDFs for features such as stamps, tables and field boundaries, preparing documents for extraction. In the MVP, users select whether a document is an invoice, packing list or ID; classification is therefore user-selected, not autonomous. Extracted information populates standardized Excel or PDF templates that users can review and edit before submission. [PDF p. 2]

Access controls and stored data

The described implementation includes end-to-end encryption for database data, including names and identity numbers, plus individual user logins managed through an admin panel. [PDF p. 2]

Results and Business Impact

The case study estimates up to 90% reduction in processing time compared with prior manual transcription, and reports 97% fewer human errors relative to manual entry. These are source-reported estimates and claims, not independently validated production results. Absolute processing times, error counts, evaluation sample and measurement protocol were not provided, so the business impact cannot be quantified beyond those reported figures. [PDF p. 3] [PDF p. 1]

The MVP architecture is described as designed to support up to 1,000 declarants; this is a design target, not evidence of active users or observed adoption. [PDF p. 3]

Limitations and Next Steps

Classification remains a user action in the MVP. Automated document classification, deeper ERP integrations and declaration generation without manual review are identified as Phase 2 roadmap items, not delivered capabilities. The reported material does not provide evaluation conditions or independent validation for the time and error figures. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 3]

No additional next steps or deployment/adoption measurements were provided. Publication and owner approval are pending; image permissions are also pending, so no source image is included.

Conclusion

The MVP applies local OCR, computer vision and LLM extraction to customs documents, producing editable declaration templates while keeping processing within the client’s infrastructure. It is an assisted workflow with human-selected document types and review before submission; further automation remains roadmap work. The reported efficiency and error figures should be read as unvalidated source-reported estimates, not established business results. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 3]
