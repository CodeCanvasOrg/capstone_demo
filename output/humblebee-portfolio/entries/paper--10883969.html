Practical Evaluation Framework for Real-Time Multi-Object Tracking: Achieving Optimal and Realistic Performance

Practical Evaluation Framework for Real-Time Multi-Object Tracking: Achieving Optimal and Realistic Performance
A published research study evaluates multi-object tracking across detection, re-identification and tracking under practical runtime constraints.

Executive Summary

This research entry covers a framework for evaluating multi-object tracking (MOT) systems for practitioners concerned with accuracy and real-time operation, including edge-device scenarios. The paper, “Practical Evaluation Framework for Real-Time Multi-Object Tracking: Achieving Optimal and Realistic Performance,” by Jumabek Alikhanov, Dilshod Obidov, Mirsaid Abdurasulov and Hakil Kim, was published in IEEE Access, volume 13, in 2025. It asks how tracking should be evaluated under practical detector, ReID and runtime constraints. This is research, not evidence of a named product deployment or client result. Publication record: IEEE Access paper. [PDF p. 1]

Context and Challenge

MOT systems must maintain object identities across video frames, but appearance-based ReID can add computational load through dedicated models and processing. The paper argues that evaluations focused on selected benchmarks or tracker components may miss real deployment costs, especially when preprocessing and detector settings affect runtime and accuracy. It therefore treats detection, ReID and tracking updates as a complete pipeline rather than evaluating the tracker in isolation. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 3]

Technical Approach

The framework evaluates the pipeline with a consistent off-the-shelf detector and includes online processing so reported speed accounts for system components such as cropping and resizing. It examines HOTA for tracking accuracy and frames per second (FPS) for runtime. LITE (Lightweight Integrated Tracking-Feature Extraction) supplies appearance descriptors from intermediate object-detector features, avoiding a separate ReID inference model. The study also evaluates ReID match discrimination through positive and negative appearance-match scores. [PDF p. 3] [PDF p. 5]

Implementation

Experiments use YOLOv8m as the same generic detector across trackers and compare original trackers with LITE variants. Reported datasets include MOT17, MOT20, PersonPath22, KITTI and VIRAT-S. The paper reports that LITE integrates detector features into tracking; no named product, production deployment or client integration is established by the reviewed evidence. [PDF p. 8] [PDF p. 5]

Evaluation

Evaluation compares HOTA and FPS across five datasets using the common YOLOv8m detector; the framework also considers detector input resolution and confidence thresholds. The paper reports HOTA “up to 30%” with resolution and threshold customization versus 10% without optimization. This is a configuration-dependent research finding, not a universal 20-percentage-point uplift. The reviewed source record does not provide the test hardware, exact workload details, FPS values or complete conditions for that comparison, so those measurements cannot be independently contextualized here. [PDF p. 8] [PDF p. 1]

Results and Business Impact

Technically, the study reports that LITE-enabled ReID trackers operate at similar speeds to motion-only systems without compromising accuracy, and that detector-setting choices can materially affect HOTA. No quantified business impact, client deployment outcome or production result has been provided; the paper’s metrics are research findings, not client results. [PDF p. 1]

Limitations and Next Steps

The reported HOTA comparison is configuration-dependent, and test hardware and detailed workload conditions are not available in the reviewed record. The paper identifies adaptive parameter selection and improved matching strategies that better use ReID features as future research directions; these are proposals, not delivered capabilities. Product availability and deployment status are not established. [PDF p. 16]

Conclusion

The study provides a practical evaluation perspective: measure the full MOT pipeline, account for detector configuration and assess ReID’s runtime contribution rather than relying on isolated tracker benchmarks. Its findings support more realistic research comparisons, while deployment performance and business outcomes remain unreported. [PDF p. 3] [PDF p. 1]
