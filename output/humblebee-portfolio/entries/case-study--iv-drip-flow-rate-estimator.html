IV Drip Flow Rate Estimator

IV Drip Flow Rate Estimator: an offline mobile computer-vision application that estimates IV drip flow rates from a phone camera for nursing teams.

Executive Summary

A medical technology provider (unnamed) worked with HumbleBeeAI on a mobile application intended to help healthcare professionals estimate IV drip flow rates. Manual drop counting takes staff attention and may vary with distraction, fatigue, lighting and observer skill. The case-study describes an iOS/Android camera-based estimator that operates without internet access. Its reported measurements are source claims, not independently validated; deployment and production status have not been provided. [PDF p. 1]Original case study: https://humblebee.ai/case-studies/iv-drip-flow-rate-estimator

Context and Challenge

Traditionally, nursing staff count drops manually while monitoring IV delivery. The source says this requires continuous attention and can produce inconsistent readings, particularly amid distraction or fatigue; visibility, lighting and differences in observer skill are also cited as sources of variation. Staff may spend up to 3 minutes per observation, according to the case-study. The source does not provide a matched comparison protocol or clarify the settings in which those conditions were assessed. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 2]

Solution and Capabilities

Camera-based flow-rate estimation

The delivered capability described is an application for iOS and Android that uses a device camera to estimate IV flow rate. A healthcare professional points the phone at the IV setup; the app displays an estimate within seconds. The source describes the operation as real time and offline, supporting use where internet access is unavailable. [PDF p. 1]

Designed to reduce manual counting

By automating the drop-counting step, the application is intended to reduce the time and attention required for a manual observation and provide a consistent estimate. The case-study describes support for on-site healthcare staff, including in hospitals, clinics and remote facilities; actual production availability in those environments is not specified.

Results and Business Impact

The case-study reports over 95% accuracy for the camera-based, offline iOS/Android application and results within 15 seconds, compared with manual observations taking up to 3 minutes. It also characterizes the speed difference as over 10x faster than manual counting. These are reported measurements, not independently validated results. The source gives no accuracy tolerance, reference instrument, validation sample, lighting or device test matrix, or matched-pair basis for the speed comparison. The supplied record does not establish measured clinical outcomes or patient benefit; no business-impact measurement has been provided. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 2]
