How Johnson and Johnson Found Patterns Hidden in Ablation Data?

How Johnson & Johnson used CARTONET to organize AVNRT ablation data and explore patterns across procedures [PDF p. 1]

Executive Summary

HumbleBeeAI developed a custom analytical framework for Johnson & Johnson to address cardiac ablation analysis in AVNRT procedures. Procedure records were difficult to align and interpret across patients; CARTONET was built to normalize the data, support statistical analysis, and provide interactive 3D views. The source reports improved consistency and analysis efficiency, but gives no numerical performance measure. This portfolio entry is based on a reviewed website snapshot, with owner approval pending. [PDF p. 1] [PDF p. 2]

Context and Challenge

The project addressed a data-structure problem, not a lack of clinical data. Files for AVNRT procedures included XLSX and CSV records, while patient-specific 3D mapping coordinates differed and the tabular data could contain inconsistent categories, free-text annotations, or missing codes. As a result, cross-patient comparison depended on manual cleanup and was difficult to perform consistently. The source describes these constraints but does not quantify the number of procedures, users, or time spent in the prior workflow. [PDF p. 2] [PDF p. 2]

Solution and Capabilities

Data normalization for comparison

CARTONET ingested XLSX and CSV files and aligned datasets to a common spatial reference using UUID identifiers and reference-based normalization anchored on His signal coordinates. This approach was intended to make comparisons across patients possible despite differing coordinate systems. [PDF p. 2]

Statistical summaries and 3D views

The framework computed summaries for measures including power, temperature, impedance drop, and spatial distances, and presented distributions and comparisons through box plots, percentiles, and charts. Interactive 3D models and LAO and RAO projections enabled users to inspect spatial relationships among His signals, ablation sites, and outcome categories, with rotation and category filtering described in the source. [PDF p. 2]

Predictive analysis

The source also describes correlations between pre-ablation features and outcomes as providing early insight into possible predictors of procedural success. It does not provide predictive validation results, test conditions, or evidence of clinical-outcome effects; this should be treated as exploratory analysis rather than validated clinical guidance.
