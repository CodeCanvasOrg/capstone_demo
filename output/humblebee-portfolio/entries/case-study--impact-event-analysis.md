# How Johnson & Johnson Turned a Mountain of Event Data into a Clear Story of ROI?

HumbleBeeAI built a Power BI analytics solution to help Johnson & Johnson’s event and commercial teams compare event activity with medical procedure-volume data. [Read the case study: “How Johnson & Johnson Turned a Mountain of Event Data into a Clear Story of ROI?”](https://humblebee.ai/case-studies/impact-event-analysis) [PDF source: 08738728-0692-4692-9c93-c63adb938233]

## Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson invests in educational and promotional events for medical professionals, but its teams needed a clearer way to assess whether those events aligned with changes in procedure volumes. HumbleBeeAI’s case study describes a Power BI solution that brings event, account and procedure data together for analysis. The case-study PDF has been reviewed, while owner approval remains pending; the source reports improved event-effectiveness analysis and ROI visibility, but gives no quantified return or causal validation. [PDF source: 5ac8ab56-6a4a-41ce-b023-aa141162e522] [PDF source: e29717b2-8eac-4ac1-bbbd-dd990ebda74e]

## Context and Challenge

Event records were isolated from procedure data, leaving teams without a unified basis for examining event performance. The source also describes missing account codes, inconsistent formatting and hours spent on manual cleanup, alongside limited regional comparisons and a lack of benchmarks between accounts that hosted events and those that did not. The intended users needed an approach that could accommodate thousands of records and remain accessible to non-technical stakeholders; no detailed test conditions or dataset scale are reported. [PDF source: 00a8abb0-d589-4675-be0f-54a904e80e02]

## Solution and Capabilities

### Data integration and analysis

The case study describes Power Query cleaning and merging account records, event logs and procedure data, including filling missing codes, resolving duplicates and correcting inconsistencies. Within Power BI, the team compared procedure volumes before and after events at account level, contrasted event-hosting accounts with non-event accounts, and mapped utilization geographically. These views are comparative analyses; they do not by themselves establish that events caused procedure changes. [PDF source: 0792e0c6-e0b2-4f71-8a9c-2fb01a2736b6] [PDF source: 426fc694-5cea-4412-b122-61ff93d0753d]

### Interactive dashboards

Delivered dashboards let stakeholders filter results by account, event type, procedure or timeframe and explore the data through drill-downs. This was intended to support independent exploration without technical support; the source provides no usability-test results or quantified time savings. [PDF source: a505591f-83d7-46fa-941b-a50a04a6ab10]

## Results and Business Impact

The source reports clearer visibility into event ROI, account-level comparisons and regional performance, and says teams could use the views to identify accounts where procedure volumes rose after events. It also reports improved event-effectiveness analysis and ROI visibility. However, it provides no numerical ROI, effect size, quantified operational savings or causal validation. No comparison period, sample size, test conditions or measured business-impact figures are supplied, so the reported observations should not be read as proof that events caused procedure growth. ## Limitations and Next Steps

The source does not provide numerical outcome measures, validation methodology, detailed implementation or performance-test conditions. Although it describes the analytics solution and dashboards as delivered, the available evidence does not establish causal clinical impact or quantify ROI. ## Conclusion

The project brings event and procedure information into a Power BI environment for account, event and regional comparisons, giving Johnson & Johnson’s teams a more structured basis for reviewing event effectiveness. The supported value is improved visibility and analysis—not a quantified return or demonstrated causal effect.
